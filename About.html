About the data behind this map.

Every layer in this tool is sourced from public data or NFP operational records. This page documents each source, its vintage, known caveats, and the transformations we applied to get it onto the map. Read this before citing a number in a grant proposal or a board meeting.

Data sources

The map harmonizes six datasets at two geographic grains — 2020 Census tracts (434 tracts) and 2020 ZCTAs — clipped to the 14-county Nashville MSA.

Census

American Community Survey · 5-Year Estimates

ACS 2020–2024
Variables
Median household income (B19013), poverty rate (B17001), total population (B01003), race/ethnicity (B03002)
Geography
Census tract, 2020 vintage boundaries
Retrieval
Census Bureau API, pulled quarterly via the ACS ETL notebook
Margins
5-year estimates have smaller margins than 1-year, but tract-level income can still have ±$8k–$15k error at 90% confidence
CDC

PLACES: Local Data for Better Health

CDC PLACES 2024 release
Variables
Diabetes, high blood pressure (BPHIGH) and obesity (BMI ≥ 30) prevalence — crude prevalence, adults 18+
Geography
Census tract — model-based small-area estimates
Method
CDC uses BRFSS responses + ACS covariates to produce tract-level prevalence. These are modeled, not measured.
Caveat
PLACES estimates are a smoothed surface. Do not interpret tract-level differences below ~1 pct point as meaningful.
USDA

Food Access Research Atlas

FARA 2019 · 2010 tract boundaries
Variables
LILATracts_1And10, lapop1, lalowi1, vehicle-availability flags
LILA definition
Low-income tract (poverty rate ≥20%, or median family income ≤80% of the statewide or metro-area median) where ≥500 residents or ≥33% of the population live >1 mile (urban) or >10 miles (rural) from the nearest supermarket. Low-income residents (lalowi1) are those at or below 200% of the federal poverty level.
Boundary issue
FARA uses 2010 tract boundaries. We crosswalk to 2020 tracts — see below.
Next refresh
USDA has not published a revised FARA since 2019. We flag this on the map.
Feeding America

Map the Meal Gap · Modeled to Census Tracts

MMG 2025 method · 2023 data
Variables
Food insecurity rate (%) and estimated food-insecure residents per tract (rate × tract population)
Geography
Census tract only — no ZIP view
Method
Feeding America's county food-insecurity rates, distributed to tracts with the Map the Meal Gap 2025 regression coefficients and ACS predictors. Tract estimates re-aggregate to each county's published rate.
Caveat
These are modeled estimates. Version 1 uses overall poverty in place of non-student poverty, which can overstate food insecurity in student-heavy tracts.
CFMT

Giving Matters · Nonprofit Registry

Scraped Feb 2026
Records
1,045 middle-TN nonprofits with addresses and mission statements
Geocoding
Census Geocoder → fallback to Nominatim; 94.2% geocoded successfully
Classification
Mission statements classified by an Anthropic Claude model (claude-opus-4-7, scripts/classify_giving_matters.py) with a 16-category rubric (see below).
License
Public registry data · Community Foundation of Middle TN
NFP

Partner Network · Internal Records

Jun 2026 snapshot

43 active partner sites with meal-distribution programs

Processing pipeline

Raw sources land in /data/raw. They pass through a four-step pipeline before the map sees them.

Step 01
Ingest
Fetch from APIs or scraped registry; normalize column names; tag with vintage.
Step 02
Harmonize
Crosswalk geographies to 2020 tracts; uppercase GEOIDs to 11 digits.
Step 03
Classify
LLM categorizes nonprofit mission statements into 10 service categories.
Step 04
Publish
Write slim GeoJSON + indicator CSVs to /public/data.

USDA LILA 2010 → 2020 crosswalk

USDA's Food Access Research Atlas is still reported on 2010 census tracts, while every other dataset in this tool is on 2020 tracts. A direct join loses ~18% of tract-level LILA flags.

Our approach

  • Start with the Census 2010→2020 tract relationship file (TIGER).
  • Where a 2010 tract maps 1:1 to a 2020 tract, copy flags directly.
  • Where a 2010 tract splits into multiple 2020 tracts, propagate the LILA flag to all children.
  • Where multiple 2010 tracts merge into one 2020 tract, apply the flag if any parent was LILA (conservative — favors identifying need).
  • Population variables (lapop1, lalowi1) are apportioned by area-weighted overlap.
Caveat. This crosswalk introduces 3–6% error at the tract level. For county-level aggregates the error is negligible. For tract-level planning, treat LILA flags as indicative, not definitive.

Evaluation. 150 randomly-sampled classifications were human-reviewed in March 2026, against the earlier 10-category rubric: 91.3% agreement. The 16-category rubric (June 2026) has not yet been re-evaluated. The most confused pair is community_meals ↔ homeless_outreach, which often overlap in practice.

Known caveats

  • Vintage mismatch. USDA LILA is from 2019. ACS is 2020–24. CDC PLACES is 2024. We show the most recent available but readers should know they don't all describe the same year.
  • Modeled vs measured. PLACES diabetes rates are modeled, not observed. Treat as a directional signal, not a count of cases.
  • Suppressed tracts. ACS suppresses estimates for tracts with very small populations. These appear as "—" on the map.
  • LILA boundary error. 3–6% tract-level error from the 2010→2020 crosswalk. See above.
  • Partner registry is a snapshot. Giving Matters changes weekly; we refresh quarterly. Some listed orgs may have closed or merged.
  • Not a census of food assistance. Not every food-providing org is in Giving Matters. Informal, church-based, and mutual-aid networks are under-represented.
